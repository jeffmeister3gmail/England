The Course

"Within thirty miles of coastline lies the greatest concentration of links golf on earth."

The stretch of land between the Wirral Peninsula and Southport contains a density of world-class links golf found nowhere else. Two Open Championship venues of the very highest order. One of the most historically significant courses in the amateur game. And three clubs that would be the jewels of any other county in England.

What unites all six is the wind. Respect it — and you will have the time of your golfing life.

Hillside Golf Club

The Hidden Gem of the Sefton Coast

Overview

Hillside Golf Club sits immediately adjacent to Royal Birkdale and is widely regarded as one of the finest courses in Britain that has not yet hosted The Open Championship. The course winds through dramatic linksland, towering sand dunes, and pine-lined avenues that give it an almost dual personality: pure seaside links on the back nine and a more sheltered, woodland-tinged character on the front.

History & Heritage

Founded in 1911, Fred Hawtree's significant redesign in 1967 transformed the back nine, routing it through the dune ridges and creating several holes regarded among the most beautiful in England. The course regularly acts as a final qualifying venue for The Open Championship when held at Birkdale.

Royal Lytham & St Annes Golf Club

Where Open History Was Written in Red Brick and Rough

Overview

Royal Lytham & St Annes is one of the most storied venues in golf — an inland links surrounded by Victorian red-brick housing that delivers pure links golf through tight fairways, cavernous bunkers, and unpredictable bounces. The course has hosted The Open Championship eleven times and produced some of the most dramatic moments in the tournament's history.

History & Heritage

Founded in 1886, the club received its Royal designation in 1926 — the same year Bobby Jones won the first of his two Opens here, famously holing from a sandy lie at the 17th. Harry Colt's strategic bunkering philosophy is very much in evidence today across more than 200 bunkers.

Fun Facts & Folklore

The only Open Championship venue that begins with a par-3 — the 1st hole plays at around 206 yards, a brutal opener under pressure.
Seve Ballesteros memorably drove into a car park adjacent to the 16th in 1979, took a free drop, and made par on his way to victory.
The course has over 200 bunkers — Harry Colt's philosophy was that every wayward shot should be punished, and Lytham delivers.
A bronze plaque on the 17th fairway marks the precise spot where Bobby Jones played his legendary recovery shot from a sandy lie in 1926.
The 1961 Ryder Cup was held here, won by the USA 14.5–9.5 — it remains the last time the match was played at Lytham.
Bob Charles, who won here in 1963, remains the only left-handed player ever to win The Open Championship.

Royal Liverpool Golf Club

The Birthplace of British Golf's Grand Stage

Overview

Royal Liverpool — universally known as Hoylake — is one of the oldest and most revered links courses in England. Flat, exposed, and utterly unforgiving to the errant shot, it compensates with subtlety, history, and a bone-dry surface that makes club selection a constant intellectual exercise.

History & Heritage

Founded in 1869 on the site of a former racecourse, Hoylake hosted the very first Amateur Championship in 1885. It went nearly 40 years without hosting The Open before a triumphant return in 2006, where Tiger Woods claimed his 11th major in one of his most controlled and cerebral performances.

Fun Facts & Folklore

Tiger Woods used his 2-iron and 3-wood off nearly every tee in 2006, barely touching his driver all week — and still won by two shots.
Bobby Jones won here in 1930 as part of his legendary Grand Slam — the only player ever to hold all four major titles simultaneously.
The internal out-of-bounds 'cop' — a raised bank running along several holes — is one of the most distinctive and penal features in links golf anywhere in the world.
Brian Harman's 2023 wire-to-wire victory was one of the most dominant Open performances in decades, winning by 6 shots.
Hoylake has hosted 13 Open Championships and is one of the oldest clubs in England, founded in 1869.
The course has been stretched to over 7,300 yards for modern Opens, yet its strategic complexity remains rooted in 19th-century origins.

West Lancashire Golf Club

England's Unsung Links Masterpiece

Overview

West Lancashire is one of England's oldest clubs and most underrated links courses. Sitting on the banks of the Mersey Estuary with views across to Wales on a clear day, it offers a pure, unvarnished links experience. The fairways ripple and tumble across natural ground, and the wind turns a good score into a genuine achievement.

History & Heritage

Founded in 1873, West Lancashire is among the oldest golf clubs in England. The course occupies a spectacular stretch of coastline where the estuary meets the Irish Sea. C.K. Cotton's mid-20th century routing refinements helped consolidate the course's character, and the club maintains a proud tradition of amateur golf.

West Lancashire has long resisted commercialisation, preserving an atmosphere that feels refreshingly member-focused and unspoiled.
Despite its relative anonymity outside golfing circles, West Lancashire consistently appears in top-100 lists for the British Isles.

Southport & Ainsdale Golf Club

The Ryder Cup's Forgotten Home

Overview

Southport & Ainsdale — known as S&A — is a classic James Braid links between Hillside and Formby on the Sefton Coast. Braid's routing is intelligent and varied, mixing holes that run with and against the prevailing wind. The duneland is genuine and dramatic, and the course has a proud history that includes two Ryder Cups.

History & Heritage

Founded in 1906, the club commissioned legendary five-time Open champion James Braid to redesign the course in 1923 — and the result largely survives today. S&A hosted the Ryder Cup in 1933 (GB&I won, 6½–5½) and again in 1937 (USA won, 8–4, with Byron Nelson and Sam Snead both making their Ryder Cup debuts).

Royal Birkdale Golf Club

England's Premier Open Championship Venue

Overview

Royal Birkdale is widely considered the finest links course in England and one of the great golf venues in the world. The course is a masterwork of routing — fairways run along the valleys between the dunes, eliminating unpredictable side-slopes and creating a test that is, above all, fair. Every bad shot at Birkdale is the golfer's fault.

History & Heritage

Founded in 1889, the club received its Royal title in 1951. The 1931 redesign by Fred W. Hawtree and J.H. Taylor established the essential character of the modern course. The 1969 Ryder Cup famously ended in a tie after Jack Nicklaus conceded Tony Jacklin's putt on the final green — one of sport's greatest acts of sportsmanship.

Fun Facts & Folklore

Jack Nicklaus's concession of Tony Jacklin's putt at the 18th in 1969 — "I don't think you would have missed it, but I was not prepared to give you the chance" — is one of sport's most celebrated acts of sportsmanship.
Arnold Palmer hit a rescue shot from beneath a bush at the 16th during the 1961 Open and made a remarkable birdie. A plaque marks the exact spot to this day.
The 1935 art deco clubhouse is Grade II* listed — the asterisk denoting buildings of exceptional interest, a step above standard listed status.
Birkdale's fairways run in the valleys between the dunes rather than over them, eliminating random bounces and making it the most consistently fair test in links golf.
Jordan Spieth's 2017 final round included one of the most extraordinary recoveries in Open history — he disappeared entirely into the dunes at the 13th before holing a chip to save par.
Royal Birkdale is consistently ranked in the world's top 10 courses and is widely regarded as England's finest links.
